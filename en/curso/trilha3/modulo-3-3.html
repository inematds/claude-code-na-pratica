MODULE 3.3

💼 Monetization: turn your skill into revenue

Here we talk about revenue models, not promises of profit. You’ll learn how to find the right bottleneck, structure services, price thoughtfully, prospect respectfully, and charge professionally with Stripe. None of the figures here are guaranteed — results depend on execution, the market, and consistency.

Detailed content

1

🎯 Find the constraint before opening Claude Code

In any operation, there is one bottleneck that limits the total result. Optimizing everything that no is the bottleneck doesn’t change the final number. Before applying AI, you need to know where to aim—or you’ll become “busy but stuck,” with lots of movement and no progress.

What it is:

Identify, with one question, the single bottleneck most limiting revenue today.

Why learn:

AI is a high-impact lever; the result depends on where you point out, not how much you produce.

Key concepts:

Theory of constraints · 3-month question · "business first, AI second".

🏭 The car factory analogy

An automaker makes 100 tires and paints 500 parts per day, but assembles only one engine per day. No matter how many more tires it makes: the factory delivers one car per day, because the engine is the constraint. To grow, you work on the engine—not the tires.

🔮 The crystal ball question

Look three months ahead. If one single thing could be true within that timeframe and make everything else irrelevant, what would it be? "I have a steady flow of qualified customers." "My churn has dropped to nearly zero." That answer is your constraint — it's what the AI and your time should focus on.

2

🔁 The loop: more → better → new

Once you find the constraint, you always follow the same sequence. The order matters: only open a new channel after exhausting "more" and "better." Before that, a new channel is a distraction — several simultaneous fronts tend to delay things, not speed them up.

What it is:

An improvement cycle: restrição → mais → melhor → novo → IA acelera → medir → recomeça.

Why learn:

Growing based on what already works is the fastest and cheapest path; there’s no need to reinvent anything.

Key concepts:

Volume · quality · new channel last · focus on one offer/one client.

+

More — double down on what already works

Already closing deals through referrals? Ask for twice as many referrals. Already publishing 3 pieces of content/week? Try 6. Ask: "what's stopping me from doubling this?" and remove that friction.

↑

Better—improve the quality of the same thing

When you can’t do more, do it better: a better hook, a clearer offer, a more polished delivery. Every week, set aside the best and worst results and ask what the best ones have in common.

★

New — only then, a new channel

Have you gotten more done, and done it better? Then open a new front. This is the step you're most tempted to start too early—and the one that causes the most distraction when you do it at the wrong time.

💡 Why "one thing only"

As a market benchmark (not a revenue promise): you can build a solid business by focusing on one offer for one type of customer, instead of working on five at the same time. Focus speeds things up; distraction slows things down.

3

🩺 The ladder: audit → build → retainer

Don’t pitch a big project right away. Start with a small, paid entry-level offer — an audit. The client pays little, sees the value, and is much more likely to make a bigger commitment later. The website is often the way in: visible, useful, and quick to deliver with Claude Code.

What it is:

A sequence of offers that increases commitment: paid diagnosis → delivery → recurring service.

Why learn:

Someone who delivers a website rarely becomes "the website person": they become "the AI person" the client calls for everything.

Key concepts:

Entry-level offer · gateway · from executor to partner · productize what repeats.

Tier 1

🔍 Audit

Small, paid diagnostic. Maps the constraint using the client’s numbers.

Tier 2

🧱 Building

The deliverable: a simple, good, fast website/landing page/automation that addresses the constraint.

Tier 3

🔁 Retainer

Recurring work: improvements and new automations as the client's "AI person".

📦 When productizing

Same solution + same profile + repetition = product. When you’ve already delivered something similar 3+ times, standardize scope, timeline, and price, and turn custom work into a fixed package with its own checklist. Better margins, less effort per sale.

Ladder map: Audit (paid entry point) → Build (delivery) → Retainer (recurring revenue) → +1% each cycle.

4

💰 Price based on value: the cost of inaction

Price doesn't come from your costs; it comes from value to the customer. And the most honest way to make this value visible is to calculate the cost of inaction — how much the problem costs while it remains unresolved—using the client’s own numbers, never made-up figures.

What it is:

Anchor the price to the value generated or lost for the customer, not your hours.

Why learn:

When the cost of the problem is quantified, the price of the solution starts to make sense on its own.

Key concepts:

Cost of inaction · 80/20 · client numbers · no promise of gains.

✓ Value-based pricing

  • ✓"How much do you earn per customer, on average?" → makes the cost of the problem visible.
  • ✓Calculate it using the numbers from the client ("if you’re short ~10 customers/month...").
  • ✓The price reflects the value—and you set the range for your market.

✗ What to avoid

  • ✗Promise earnings or "guaranteed returns"—never.
  • ✗Make up numbers for the cost of inaction to inflate the sale.
  • ✗Charging only by the hour hides the real value and ties you to your time.

🎯 Realistic expectations

Revenue models aren’t revenue forecasts. You structure the offer and price thoughtfully; the result depends on execution, the market, and consistency. Talking about this honestly is part of your positioning.

5

📣 Prospect respectfully: attention is the asset

Where attention goes, money follows. The best clients come through inbound: you provided value publicly first, so they came already trusting you — and people who are sought out negotiate better than those who go looking. But inbound takes time; the respectful outbound is the fastest way to get started.

What it is:

Build a public presence (content/portfolio) that generates demand over time, alongside active prospecting.

Why learn:

Clients who already trust you are cheaper to close and negotiate better with you.

Key concepts:

Value × reach · inbound vs. outbound · social proof · one sustainable channel.

🧲 Inbound (builds trust)

  • •Publish real results (sites/automations you’ve built) in a useful and honest way.
  • •Choose one channel you can truly sustain for months.
  • •Slower to build, but it shifts the power dynamic in your favor.

📨 Outbound (quick at first)

  • •DMs, email, and active outreach — respectful, no spam.
  • •Fastest path to your first customers; harder to sustain.
  • •Start here as you gradually build the presence that generates inbound leads.
6

💳 Deliver and charge: cadence and Stripe

Billing frequency changes retention. The more less frequent the cycle (monthly < quarterly < annual), the longer it tends to be, the longer the customer stays; weekly billing usually leads to more cancellations. To charge professionally, use the Stripe: one-time for the audit, subscription for the retainer.

What it is:

Structure one-time and recurring charges, and choose the cadence that sustains the relationship.

Why learn:

The right cadence protects lifetime value; the wrong one leaks revenue through cancellations.

Key concepts:

Lifetime value · Products & Prices · Payment Link · subscription · automatic receipt.

Stripe setup guide (use only the official documentation: docs.stripe.com):

  • 1.Create Products e Prices: one-time for the audit, recurring for the retainer.
  • 2.Generate a Payment Link for the audit and a subscription for recurring revenue.
  • 3.Favor longer retainer cycles; offer an honest discount for annual plans.
  • 4.Configure automatic receipts and, if applicable, clear trial and cancellation terms.
Going deeper (optional): why long cycles retain more

Each renewal is a decision point for the customer—and every decision is a chance to cancel. Fewer renewals per year mean fewer chances to leave and more time for value to build. That’s why an annual plan (with an honest discount) usually retains customers better than a monthly plan, and a monthly plan better than a weekly one.

Self-check (optional): what billing cadence tends to retain more on the retainer?

🧭 Guided Hands-On Example

Scenario: local service provider (studio, clinic, repair shop) who says "I don't have enough customers."

1. Diagnostic conversation (~15 min)

Why are we talking → what's the business's biggest problem → cost of inaction (using the client's numbers) → propose an audit as a small, paid entry-level offer.

2. Audit delivery

Post-conversation questionnaire → call to present the findings and the constraint → close with two options: A) Maintenance and B) Growth. The client chooses during the call.

3. Building and Retention

If the finding is "needs a website/landing page," build it quickly with Claude Code (Track 2). Position yourself as "the AI person": close a quarterly/annual retainer. In Stripe: offer the audit as a one-time purchase (Payment Link) and the retainer as a subscription.

✏️ Exercises

1. Find your constraint (easy)

Answer the 3-month question in writing for your business (or target client) and write “My constraint is ___.” Criterion: one specific, measurable sentence.

2. "More → better → new" plan (medium)

For your biggest source of customers, write down one action to do more of, one to do better, and why you won’t open a new channel right now. Criterion: the 3 decisions on one page, with “new” justifiably postponed.

3. Diagnostic Script (medium)

Write the 15-minute conversation script for a real profile. Criterion: the "cost of inaction" is calculated using the client's numbers, with no invented values.

4. Build your offer ladder (medium)

Define the scope, timeline, and price range for the 3 tiers, in line with your market. Criterion: a 3-row table with scope and pricing you defined (not copied).

5. Stripe billing (test) (practical)

In test mode, create a one-time product (audit) with a Payment Link and a subscription product (retainer). Criterion: a working test payment link and a test subscription created.

⚠️ Common errors

  • ✗Applying AI before finding the bottleneck. Build a thousand cool tools that don’t touch the constraint. Business first, AI second.
  • ✗Opening a new channel too early. Skip “more” and “better” and spread yourself across five fronts. Exhaust what already works first.
  • ✗Skip straight to the big sale. High-value pitch without the entry-level offer. Without trust built, conversion plummets.
  • ✗Weekly billing “to ensure cash flow.” Very frequent billing increases cancellations. For a retainer, prefer monthly/quarterly/annual billing.
  • ✗Promise earnings or make up numbers. Never guarantee income or use made-up figures for the "cost of inaction." Talk about models, not certain outcomes.
